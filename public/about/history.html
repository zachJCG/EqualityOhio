Our History

Equality Ohio was founded in 2005 by a group of 65 dedicated LGBT activists and allies from all corners of Ohio, after voters passed a constitutional amendment prohibiting same-sex marriage and civil unions.

The Early Years

Rooted in advocacy

From those 65 founders, Equality Ohio’s work has grown broad, focusing on multiple aspects of LGBTQ equality in Ohio. A primary issue has always been the lack of inclusion of LGBTQ people in the laws that make discrimination illegal in Ohio. Just four years after our founding, we celebrated the Ohio House of Representatives passing what was then called the Equal Housing & Employment Act with 38 bipartisan co-sponsors. Unfortunately, the bill died in the Senate.

Alongside nondiscrimination work, Equality Ohio engaged in policy work to patch up the lack of inclusive laws. We worked with several cities — despite lawsuits — to establish domestic partnership registries, including two in public school systems. These registries gave local employers a way of officially determining who was eligible for company benefits. Once the registries were created, we supported revising tax structures which imposed additional burdens on same-sex couples.

2013–2015

Winning hearts and minds

Why Marriage Matters Ohio

In September of 2013, Equality Ohio Education Fund, ACLU Foundation of Ohio, and Human Rights Campaign Foundation launched Why Marriage Matters Ohio to win the hearts and minds of Ohioans for marriage equality. In just a few short years, we moved Ohioans from a 47% favorability of marriage equality to 53%.

When the Obergefell ruling was issued, Why Marriage Matters Ohio readied probate courts and held rallies outside of them, with clergy on hand to marry people. Ultimately, we gathered the visible support of over 300 faith leaders throughout Ohio for marriage equality.

The Gay Games

In 2014, Cleveland+Akron hosted the Gay Games — open to all, and specifically meant to be open and welcoming to LGBTQ people. Connie Schultz, who received a Pulitzer for, among other works, a piece on same-sex marriage, and whom we honored as an Ally of the Year, spoke passionately about LGBTQ equality in the opening ceremony. Equality Ohio secured the participation of nearly 100 elected leaders in Gay Games ceremonies and events.

2015 Onward

Building the modern fight

Ohio Business Competes

Support from the business community began to increase as a result of the push for marriage equality. In August of 2015, Equality Ohio, ACLU of Ohio, and Human Rights Campaign started Ohio Business Competes — a no-cost, nonpartisan coalition of corporations, universities, and law firms that are supportive of LGBTQ-inclusive nondiscrimination policy at the state level.

The Ohio Fairness Act

In January of 2018, the Ohio House of Representatives gave the Ohio Fairness Act — which would add LGBTQ people to the nondiscrimination laws — proponent testimony. This was the bill’s first opportunity for public feedback in over a decade. Equality Ohio worked with the community to submit 150+ pieces of testimony, and we packed two hearing rooms.
